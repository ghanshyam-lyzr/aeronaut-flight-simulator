# Aeronaut styleguide

## Current UI direction

Alpine Flight — a cinematic browser flight simulator whose landscape dominates a compact, precise flight deck. Adapted from the dealt true-black-flat direction (seed recorded in .taste-skill), with neutral charcoal surfaces and a single chartreuse signal color.

1. Domain: a playable flight simulator for curious pilots; the first impression should be freedom and geographic scale.
2. Tone: cinematic aviation. The world carries the spectacle, while restrained flight instruments preserve visibility.
3. Palette: custom neutral graphite hsl(210 8% 9%), elevated hsl(210 6% 14%), ivory hsl(60 9% 96%), signal hsl(77 88% 67%). Adapt Ocean Deep's alpine context into the 3D water and atmosphere; keep interface colors neutral.
4. Type: DM Sans 400/500/600 for the interface; Barlow Condensed 400/500 for tabular telemetry. Restrict tracked uppercase to aviation instrument markings and wordmark.
5. Layout: full viewport canvas, a 68px top bar, 16px outer gutters, 290px corner mission panel, a compact instrument strip at the bottom. Phone uses a minimal bar, a two-row telemetry grid and touch yoke.
6. Details: 10px panels, 6px controls, one-pixel subdued instrument boundaries, opaque/translucent charcoal without backdrop blur, no ornamental gradients. Animated world and live telemetry; 160ms control transitions.
7. Name: Alpine Flight.

World is procedural; all geographic and aircraft labels describe an original fictional flight environment inspired by the Alps. No photos, downloaded models, or real-time weather data.

Final UI refinement: instrument labels use sentence case at 11px minimum. Brand uses DM Sans; telemetry uses Barlow Condensed. Controls are anchored around the full-screen world. On phones, the menu exposes Explore, Flight log, Controls, and Weather; the camera pulls back to fit the full aircraft wingspan.

Stall warnings use a compact red banner with ivory DM Sans 600 text. Reserve hsl(0 78% 40%) and hsl(0 65% 25%) for the one-second warning flash; reduced-motion mode keeps the banner steady. Position the phone warning below the mission and navigation panels and the separate GEAR banner.

Rendering refinement — Alpine Clarity: the world is lit and hazed as one system. Valley haze pools low and thins with height (peaks stand clear above it) and warms on the sun's side; it is the only fog model, shared by every fogged material. The lake reads its true depth from the terrain function: pale turquoise shelves, a deep teal basin, a thin foam lap at the shore, sky reflection by Fresnel and a restrained sun glint; fine ripples fade with distance so the far water never shimmers. Meadows stay olive with sun-cured patches, never lawn green. The sky is the aircraft's environment light (paint, glass and metal reflect it); terrain and forest keep the hemisphere light, since sampling the sky there costs more than it shows. Low quality drops the shoreline depth.

Helicopter addition — Alpine Hover: retain Alpine Flight's graphite, ivory and chartreuse instruments, DM Sans labels and Barlow Condensed telemetry in the full-world dashboard. The original Laret H4 uses an ivory cabin, rescue-red belly/fin, reflective blue-grey glazing, dark skids and signal-tipped rotors; no aircraft asset is downloaded. Keep narrow dark cabin framing and a low central live instrument pedestal so the alpine view dominates. Phone cyclic, yaw and collective controls use the existing 6px control corners, one-pixel boundaries and quiet transitions. Preserve the one-metre camera near plane.


Aircraft addition — Alpine Flight continues unchanged in the UI. The specifically requested Boeing 737-800 is the exception to the fictional-aircraft convention above. Its original procedural geometry follows the wingleted NG silhouette; ivory paint, deep teal fin/winglets and a narrow coral stripe connect it to the valley and existing fleet without reproducing an airline livery. Keep DM Sans/Barlow and existing instrument density, boundaries, spacing and motion. The larger airframe gets proportional camera distance with centred phone framing. Control surfaces, fan rotation and gear travel supply the motion; no added ornamental effects or interface palette.

Landing mission — retain Alpine Flight's graphite/ivory surfaces, chartreuse signal, DM Sans labels and Barlow Condensed telemetry. A compact corner card carries target speed, runway-relative guidance and rollout objectives; thin chartreuse world-space brackets and a runway target outline guide the approach. Phones use a full-width mission card and omit the weather/map panels while flying the mission so the cyclic and instruments stay usable. Results reuse panel tokens, 6px controls and quiet transitions, with clear touchdown measurements and retry/free-flight actions.

Fleet expansion — keep Alpine Flight's cinematic aviation tone, neutral graphite surfaces, ivory DM Sans labels, Barlow Condensed telemetry and chartreuse signal. A bounded 320px roster (280px on phones) uses compact radio rows, aircraft names and quiet category badges; its scroll position and focus survive selection. Every choice remains in the existing Explore dialog, whose heading counts the fleet. Original Mira T2, Brenta B6, Sarine C24, Orsino R70, Aven W280 and Silex A3 use ivory airframes with blue, amber, forest, slate, teal and red accents respectively. Analogue single-engine panels, twin-prop monitors and glass passenger decks stay low in the view with narrow cabin posts; the aerobatic bubble canopy has open forward glazing. Retain the one-metre near plane, centred portrait cameras and restrained motion.

Canard addition — Alpine Flight continues unchanged in the UI; the roster heading counts the fleet instead of quoting a number. The original Neris P2 uses the shared ivory airframe with a berry accent hsl(328 40% 33%) on its canard elevators, ailerons, wingtip fins and spinner, plus chartreuse rudders and blade tips. Its glazed tandem canopy runs almost to the nose. The front-seat deck keeps the low analogue panel and an open bubble with no forward posts; the canard halves and their moving elevators sit low in the view on either side. Retain the one-metre near plane, centred portrait cameras and restrained motion.

Utility addition — Alpine Flight continues unchanged in the UI. The original Veyra U9 keeps the ivory fleet airframe with an aubergine accent hsl(318 26% 29%), copper cheat line hsl(24 58% 56%) and warm-yellow prop tips; its silhouette (boxy high-wing cabin, long turbine nose, five-blade prop, cargo door and pod, fixed tricycle gear, low tail) rather than colour sets it apart. Its single-pilot deck follows the fleet cockpit: graphite panel low in the view, chartreuse live values, muted labels, narrow door posts, a slim centre windshield divider and an accent wing carry-through beam overhead.

Transport helicopter addition — Alpine Hover continues unchanged in the UI. The original Sorna H8 keeps the ivory fleet airframe with a slate lower body, sponsons and fin hsl(214 30% 27%), a thin sand cheat line hsl(38 45% 62%) and the chartreuse signal on its five blade tips; the ducted tail rotor is lipped in the same sand. Its silhouette (long rounded cabin, twin roof turbines, enclosed fin rotor, fixed wheels on sponsons) rather than colour separates it from the Laret H4. The transport deck follows the fleet cockpit: a low graphite panel with DM Sans labels, Barlow Condensed values and chartreuse bars for rotor speed, collective (with an ivory hover mark) and shared twin torque; narrow door posts, a slim windscreen divider and a slate overhead beam. The panel keeps a fixed, unsqueezed size inside the gap the HUD leaves (above the instrument strip, between the camera chip and the map); phones swap it for a compact rotor/collective/torque strip between the map and the camera chip, since the HUD already shows speed, altitude and vertical speed. Wheel wording replaces skid wording wherever this aircraft is named. Retain the one-metre near plane, centred portrait cameras and restrained motion.

Cargo transport addition — Alpine Flight's interface is unchanged. The original Montis C90 is the fleet's heavy four-engine turboprop: ivory airframe with a deep olive hsl(84 22% 30%) accent on fin, ailerons, elevators, spinners and cheatline, a pale gold hsl(44 62% 64%) pinstripe, rudder and blade tips, and dark ramp seams. Its silhouette carries the identity — broad high wing, four separate nacelles, deep boxy hold sweeping up into a rear ramp, small flight-deck panes, tall fin and gear sponsons. The transport deck keeps the low graphite panel, narrow mullions and one eyebrow frame so the valley stays dominant; four ivory-knobbed power levers and four matching engine columns are its only additions.

Powered sailplane — the original Varen G18 keeps Alpine Flight's interface unchanged: ivory airframe, indigo-violet hsl(255 28% 38%) rudder, elevators, cheatlines and engine nacelle, and high-visibility chartreuse wingtips, winglets and prop tips. Its silhouette is the long thin wing over a slender pod-and-boom fuselage, one-piece tinted canopy and T-tail. The sailplane deck sits low behind a canopy with no windshield post: airspeed, altitude, a central variometer and a quiet motor/flap/gear readout in the fleet decks' monospace canvas style on the graphite face, chartreuse needles only. The violet keeps it distinct from the Neris P2's berry accent. Motion comes from the mast prop (stopped at idle), long ailerons, flaps, elevators, rudder and rolling wheels; no added effects.

Biplane addition — Alpine Meadow: the original Alba B2 adds no interface palette. Its livery is drawn from the valley rather than the UI: olive doped fuselage hsl(82 24% 30%) echoing the sun-cured meadows, ivory fabric wings hsl(48 24% 90%) shared with the fleet, a polished aluminium cowl, and a single copper hsl(26 58% 50%) line repeated only on the upper wingtips and rudder. Matte fabric with light clearcoat, dark graphite struts and thin wires; no decals or real-aircraft markings. The open-cockpit view keeps the upper wing as a narrow overhead band and the instrument board low and compact, with Barlow Condensed dial numerals in the existing graphite/ivory/chartreuse instrument colours.
